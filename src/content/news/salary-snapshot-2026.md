Kenya's tech salary conversation runs on whispers and rumour, so this snapshot compiles the published surveys instead: CareerBuddy's June 2026 report, Mctaba's June 2026 Africa tech salary data, and WageIndicator's 2026 Kenya series. Where they disagree, we show the disagreement rather than averaging it away, a band you can defend beats a number you cannot.

The shape of the market: junior engineers land between KSh 50,000 and 130,000 per month; mid-level roles cluster at KSh 100,000–250,000; senior engineers and specialists command KSh 250,000–400,000 and beyond, especially in payments infrastructure where M-KOPA, Cellulant and Pesapal compete for the same scarce rails talent. Data and ML roles price slightly above backend at every seniority level, reflecting the continent-wide squeeze for people who can ship models into production rather than notebooks.

Two structural notes matter. First, dollar-paying remote employers set the ceiling, Microsoft's Africa Development Centre was paying well above local bands before its October 2026 closure, and its alumni are now the salary floor for senior startup hires. Second, equity remains mostly decorative at Kenyan seed stage: ESOPs exist on term sheets but cash bands do the hiring, which is why this guide tracks cash only.

For employers, the honest playbook: publish a band. The listings that convert fastest on this job board carry real ranges, and candidates increasingly treat a missing range as a missing company.

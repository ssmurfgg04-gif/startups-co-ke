On September 16, 2026, Business Daily tallied the damage: investors have lost roughly Sh93 billion across thirteen Kenyan startup failures — Twiga Foods, Copia, Koko Networks and Lipa Later among them. Twiga alone had raised $185.4M before entering administration on August 17. These are not rounding errors in a young ecosystem; they are the correction arriving all at once, four years after the 2021–22 boom priced growth at any cost.

What the correction exposes is a data problem as much as a capital problem. Ask how many startups Kenya has and the industry's own databases disagree wildly: Tracxn lists 13,642 Kenyan startups and $13.1B of lifetime venture capital; StartupBlink counts 659. Neither figure is a lie — they measure different things with different methods, and neither tells you which of the two is inflated by listings that never raised a shilling. Investors who priced rounds off the loud number rather than the verified one are the ones wearing the Sh93bn.

The fix is unglamorous: statuses on every figure. A round is confirmed when a company or registrar says so; reported when a single credible outlet carries it; estimated when we model it — and each status carries its source. On this platform the failure states are tracked like the funding states: Twiga's profile reads In administration, Sendy's reads Shut down (2023), Copia's reads In liquidation. A directory that hides its dead companies is marketing, not intelligence.

Kenya's honest numbers are still strong by any African measure: $984M raised in 2025, the continent's highest for a second straight year, before H1 2026 fell 44.5% to $126M. The discipline the ecosystem needs now is the same one this database runs on — show the source, show the date, show the status, and let the reader decide.

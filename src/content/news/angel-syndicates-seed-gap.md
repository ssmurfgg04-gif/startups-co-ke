Five years ago, a Kenyan founder raising seed had a predictable path: one of three institutional funds, a six-week process, and a term sheet with a lead. Today the path more often starts with a WhatsApp message to an angel group chair. Syndicates like Acacia Angel Network now close more sub-$250K rounds than every institutional seed fund combined (sample), and they close them in half the time.

The mechanics matter. A syndicate round is a rolling close: a well-regarded founder can bank the first $80K within days, then fill over a month. That speed is a genuine advantage in a market where a duka-credit pilot or a county contract doesn't wait for committee cycles. The trade-off is governance — thirty angels on a SAFE means thirty opinions at your next bridge.

Founders navigating this shift should do three things. Keep institutional funds warm even while closing angels, because the Series A conversation starts the day your seed closes. Paper the syndicate with a single point of contact. And treat the syndicate's operator-angels as your real product: the right one is worth more than the money.

In August 2026, Flowt (an AI startup founded by Elana Laichena that converts informal financial records into lender-ready data) announced the first close of its pre-seed round. The syndicate behind it reads like a who's-who of the new seed gap-fillers: Delta40 Fund I (climate and gender-lens), Impacc (impact), and the Argidius Foundation. None of them is a traditional Series A fund; all of them write cheques institutions no longer will.

The mechanics matter for founders. Pre-seed rounds in Kenya now routinely blend grants (foundations), soft loans and priced equity, with tranches released against milestones rather than dates. Antler's Nairobi residency continues to be a day-one founder factory, HoneyCoin, which closed a $4.9M seed in 2025, started there. The trade-off is diligence: syndicate capital is slower and more conditional, but it comes with operator support that passive capital never provided.

For investors, the lesson of the seed gap is that the best dealflow in Nairobi is now assembled, not discovered. The accelerators and studios know which founders are weeks from fundable before any database does, which is why this platform tracks accelerators as first-class entities in the investor directory.

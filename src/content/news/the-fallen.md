Twiga Foods entered administration on August 17, 2026, with an administrator appointed by the court, the end of a road that began in 2014 with a promise to fix produce distribution between Kenyan farmers and urban vendors, and $185.4M of capital later. Sendy had already shut in August 2023 after raising $20M for freight and fulfilment. Copia Global, which raised over $100M to bring e-commerce to rural consumers through agent networks, went into liquidation in September 2026, with Business Daily reporting investors are unlikely to recover much of anything.

The pattern across the three is not mystery, it is unit economics that never closed. Twiga's model carried cold chain and logistics costs that gross margin never covered at scale; Copia's agent network required density that rural purchasing power could not fund; Sendy diversified into too many lines (freight, fulfilment, essentials) as capital tightened. In each case, growth metrics led while contribution margin lagged, and follow-on capital arrived on the strength of the former.

For the ecosystem, the lesson is epistemic before it is financial. Every one of these companies looked excellent in a database that tracked raises but not contribution margin, which is to say, every database on the market. The profiles on this platform keep their failure states visible on purpose: a directory that quietly drops its dead companies teaches the next founder nothing.

What to watch next: whether the administrators can preserve Twiga's vendor network as a going concern, and whether Copia's liquidation sets a recovery precedent for investors in future Kenyan collapses. Both outcomes will shape how international LPs price Kenyan exposure for years.

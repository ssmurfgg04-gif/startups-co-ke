Hackathons are Kenya's most visible talent funnel and its least measured. We tracked 40 prize-winning teams from 2025's calendar for 18 months (sample). Thirty-one percent shipped a product that survived six months. Twelve percent raised pre-seed or grant capital. And 22% were acqui-hired — usually by the sponsor that ran the event, at a cost far below a recruiting agency's.

The pattern behind the numbers: prize money matters less than proximity. Teams that converted had three things their podium peers lacked — a design partner they met at the event, a technical lead who stayed past the demo, and a sponsor who owned a distribution channel. Mavuno Analytics is the canonical case: the Nakuru team walked away with prize money and a cooperative's phone number, and the cooperative became their first paying customer before the code was cleaned.

If you run hackathons, the implication is uncomfortable but clear: judged demos are the least valuable thing you produce. The pipeline is in the hallways. Budget accordingly — the events that feed the ecosystem are the ones that structure introductions, not just judging.

The climate money arriving in Nairobi doesn't look like venture capital, and that is the point. In the quarters we tracked, 46% of climate-labeled deals (sample) used something other than plain preferred equity: convertible grants, revenue-based advances, or — increasingly common — a grant tranche stapled to an equity round that de-risks the first 18 months of hardware deployment.

For founders this is genuinely good news with fine print. Blended structures extend runway without dilution, and climate funds bring patient LPs. But the instruments carry covenants that behave like debt: revenue-share percentages that bite in good quarters, milestone triggers tied to deployment counts, and reporting obligations that assume a monitoring team. Two founders we interviewed (sample) spent more senior time on climate-instrument compliance than on their county contracts.

The practical guidance: model the downside quarter, not the average one. A revenue-share set at 6% looks trivial until a drought quarter arrives with the payment due. Negotiate a curse-off tied to absolute revenue rather than percentage-only terms. And get the grant's reporting template before you sign — if it runs to nine pages, budget the analyst.

Everyone in Nairobi knows a founder who "opened Kigali." Fewer can tell you what it cost. We reconstructed the year-one P&L for a hypothetical 12-person B2B SaaS satellite office, using founders' shared figures (sample): the true all-in number lands around $310K — roughly 40% above the pre-expansion estimate most teams make.

The overruns cluster in four places. Work permits and compliance eat $28K and five months of founder attention. Employer-of-record fees add 12–18% to payroll if you skip local incorporation — and most teams do, for at least two quarters. Sales cycles run 1.7× longer than the Nairobi equivalent because procurement is centralised and relationship-driven. And the "regional hire" premium is real: a great Rwandan AE costs Nairobi money in year one, not the 70% founders model.

The lesson isn't "don't expand." It's sequence it. The companies that made Kigali work had three things before they filed paperwork: a Nairobi logo customer who operates regionally, a local hire designate who had sold into their target vertical, and enough gross margin to survive 18 months of sub-scale revenue. If you can't name all three, you have a pipeline, not an expansion plan.
